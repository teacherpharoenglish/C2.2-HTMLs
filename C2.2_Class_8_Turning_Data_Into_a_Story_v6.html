The mission

From numbers to a defensible narrative

A strong data story does more than describe what changed. It identifies the pattern that matters, distinguishes evidence from interpretation, and explains why the audience should care—without claiming more than the data support.

Part 1 · Language toolkit

12 expressions for precise data storytelling

Learn the meaning and the job each expression does. The goal is not to use the fanciest word; it is to choose the most accurate one.

01 · Movement

Describe the trend

02 · Comparison

Connect the metrics

outpacegrow or progress faster than something else

03 · Interpretation

Explain with care

stand outbe particularly noticeable
coincide withoccur at the same time as something else
point tosuggest a possible explanation or conclusion
cannot be attributed solely tocannot be explained by just one factor
Precision note

“Surge” and “plummet” imply marked movement; “dip” suggests a smaller decline. “Coincide with” indicates timing, not proof of cause. “Point to” introduces an interpretation, not a certainty.

Part 2 · The language rule

🚫 The Banned Words Challenge

During the practice activities, do not use these basic words or phrases to describe the data. They are not inherently wrong; the restriction forces you to be more precise.

go up go down get better get worse a lot a little big small
Worked example

“Sales went up a lot.”

“Sales surged in the final quarter.”

The revised sentence communicates both direction and magnitude more precisely.

Activity 4 · Build the story

From dashboard to narrative

Choose one dataset. Prepare a short narrative using the five-step structure below. You are not expected to explain every number—select the pattern that matters most.

1 · Context

What is being measured, and over what period?

2 · Main pattern

What changed or stands out?

3 · Turning point

When did the pattern shift?

4 · Interpretation

What might explain it? Qualify your claim.

5 · Implication

Why does it matter, and what should be checked next?

Challenge: use at least four target expressions, avoid all eight banned words, and include one sentence that distinguishes evidence from interpretation.

Activity 5 · Final speaking challenge

The Executive Briefing

Generate a briefing card. Each student receives a different professional dataset and must present a concise, evidence-based story.

Speaking rules
  • 60 seconds to prepare; 90 seconds to present.
  • Use at least four target expressions naturally.
  • Avoid all eight banned words and phrases.
  • Identify one key pattern and one possible interpretation.
  • State what the data do not prove or what you would investigate next.

Useful sentence frames
  • “What stands out is…”
  • “The most pronounced shift occurs…”
  • “This coincides with…, although the data do not establish…”
  • “Taken together, these indicators point to…”
  • “This cannot be attributed solely to…”
  • “The next step would be to establish whether…”
