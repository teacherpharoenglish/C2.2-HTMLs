The mission

Describe the relationship, not just the gap

In Class 8, you learned to turn data into a defensible narrative: observe a pattern, interpret it cautiously, and explain why it matters. In this class, the focus shifts to the exact relationship between the things being compared. Are the figures genuinely comparable? Are the outcomes disparate, merely uneven, or disproportionate? Are two patterns analogous, congruent, or virtually identical?

1 · IdentifyWhat kind of relationship does the evidence show?
2 · QualifyHow substantial, consistent or meaningful is it?
3 · DefendWhy is your chosen expression more accurate?
C2 principle: A sophisticated comparison is not necessarily a valid comparison. Before choosing the vocabulary, check whether the measures, populations, periods and denominators can legitimately be compared.
Part 1 · Language toolkit

14 expressions for precise comparison

The expressions below are not interchangeable synonyms. Each one encodes a different kind of difference, similarity or relationship. Study the distinction and notice the typical grammatical pattern.

01 · Beyond different

Difference, divergence & imbalance

disparate — markedly different in nature, type or scale; often difficult to compare directly.

The report combines disparate customer segments with very different purchasing patterns.

Pattern: disparate + plural noun
divergent — moving, developing or pointing in different directions.

Retention and acquisition followed divergent trajectories throughout the year.

Pattern: divergent trends / views / trajectories
discrepant — inconsistent; figures, records or accounts do not agree.

The finance team identified discrepant figures in the two reports.

Pattern: discrepant figures / records / accounts
uneven — irregularly distributed or inconsistent across groups, locations or periods.

Growth was uneven across the regional markets.

Pattern: uneven distribution / growth / performance
disproportionate — too large or too small in relation to something else.

The smallest region accounts for a disproportionate share of support costs.

Pattern: disproportionate to / share of
dissimilar — unlike one another in relevant characteristics, qualities or composition.

The two customer segments are dissimilar in their purchasing habits and price sensitivity.

Pattern: dissimilar to / dissimilar in + noun
contrasting — clearly different, especially when placed side by side for comparison.

The dashboard reveals contrasting growth patterns across the two regions.

Pattern: contrasting views / patterns / results
02 · Beyond the same

Similarity, equivalence & alignment

analogous — similar in function, structure or relationship, without being identical.

The pattern is analogous to the one observed in the European division.

Pattern: analogous to
comparable — sufficiently alike in relevant respects to make a meaningful comparison; not necessarily equal.

The two datasets are comparable because they use the same reporting period and methodology.

Pattern: comparable to / with
commensurate — corresponding appropriately in size, degree or proportion.

The increase in staffing was not commensurate with the rise in workload.

Pattern: commensurate with
congruent — consistent or aligned with another result, pattern, expectation or framework.

The survey findings are broadly congruent with the interview data.

Pattern: congruent with
on a par with — at the same level, standard or rank as something else.

Customer satisfaction in the northern region is now on a par with the national average.

Pattern: on a par with
tantamount to — equivalent in effect, meaning or practical significance, even if not identical in form.

With no meaningful appeal process, the policy is tantamount to an automatic rejection.

Pattern: tantamount to + noun / -ing
convergent — increasingly similar or moving towards the same point, outcome or position.

The two regions show convergent patterns of customer retention.

Pattern: convergent trends / patterns / views

Semantic focus
SAME GROUP, DIFFERENT NUANCE

Expressions in the same group are not interchangeable. Compare the relationship each one actually describes.

Beyond Different · 01
Disparate

Highlights a substantial difference in kind, nature or character.

Dissimilar

Simply indicates that two things do not share the same characteristics.

In short: disparate is generally stronger; dissimilar is more neutral.

Beyond Different · 02
Discrepant

Points to a mismatch or inconsistency between pieces of information or results.

Divergent

Describes things moving, developing or pointing in different directions.

In short: discrepant concerns inconsistency; divergent concerns separation in direction or tendency.

Beyond Different · 03
Uneven

Emphasises irregularity or a lack of balance across a distribution or period.

Disproportionate

Emphasises an imbalance relative to another figure, factor or expected scale.

In short: uneven describes an irregular pattern; disproportionate makes a relative judgement about scale.

Beyond the Same · 01
Comparable

Can be meaningfully compared, usually because a relevant basis or standard is shared.

Commensurate

Corresponds in size, degree or proportion to something else.

In short: comparable means suitable for comparison; commensurate means proportionate in scale or degree.

Beyond the Same · 02
Analogous

Shares a similar function, structure or relationship with something else.

Congruent

Is consistent with or aligned with another element, expectation or set of evidence.

In short: analogous signals resemblance; congruent signals consistency or alignment.

Beyond the Same · 03
Convergent

Describes trends, views or outcomes moving towards a shared point.

On a par with

Places something at the same level, standard or standing as another.

Also note: tantamount to means equivalent in effect or significance, not necessarily identical in form.

Decision test: Before choosing an expression, ask yourself: Am I describing a mismatch, a direction, a pattern, a valid basis for comparison, an alignment, or equivalence in effect?
Part 2 · The language rule

The Banned Words Challenge

During the speaking and writing tasks, avoid these broad comparison words. The aim is to replace them with language that identifies the precise nature of the relationship between the figures, trends or categories.

different change unlike similar the same like

Choose a more precise expression that conveys the specific relationship: for instance, whether figures are disparate, divergent, discrepant, uneven, disproportionate, dissimilar or contrasting; whether results are analogous, comparable, commensurate, congruent or convergent; or whether one is on a par with or tantamount to another.

Part 4 · Comparability audit

Can these figures legitimately be compared?

A comparison can sound convincing and still be methodologically unsound. Read each pair and decide whether the data are directly comparable, comparable only with qualification, or not comparable on the information provided.

Case A · Regional conversion

Region North: conversion rate 4.8%, calculated from completed purchases divided by unique visitors. Region South: conversion rate 5.1%, calculated from completed purchases divided by total sessions.

Case B · Survey response rates

Two departments used the same survey, identical questions, the same five-point scale and the same collection period. Department Alpha received 30 responses from 35 employees invited (86%); Department Beta received 1,008 responses from 1,200 employees invited (84%).

Case C · Customer satisfaction

Company A reports an annual satisfaction score of 82 based on a survey of verified customers. Company B reports 82 based on a survey of all website visitors, including people who have never purchased.
